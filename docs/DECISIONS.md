# Decisions

Append only. One line per decision: date, decision, why. Newest at the bottom.

- 2026-11-20: Build a browser horror game for CodeJam 16. Why: Best Game prize exists, horror demos land fast with judges.
- 2026-11-20: Dropped the government/1984 setting. Why: tone too serious.
- 2026-11-20: Chose the smart-home AI concept (working title HOMI) over the "keep it talking" PC companion. Why: the loss condition is code-controlled so prompt injection cannot win, and the goal is clearer.
- 2026-11-20: The AI never decides win or loss. Code owns all game state, the LLM only judges messages and writes dialogue. Why: anti-cheat and fairness.
- 2026-11-20: FNAF-style art (dark static images), not pixel art. Why: team preference, low animation cost.
- 2026-11-20: Game name is not final. Why: still deciding.
- 2026-11-20: No game code before Friday Nov 20, 7 PM. Why: event rule, build from scratch.
- 2026-11-20: Replaced the smart-apartment premise (HOMI, the Neighbors) with a lighthouse premise: stranded sailor, attached lighthouse AI, its ex-founder (now Ram Altman) trying to break in. Why: clearer reason to stay (stranded until rescue) and a more realistic threat. Same core loop: 3 entries, one power slot, suspicion meter.
- 2026-11-20: The lighthouse AI does not want the player dead, it wants to be needed. It withholds protection when it suspects the player is leaving. Why: removes the "wants you as keeper but wants you dead" contradiction.
- 2026-11-20: 5 nights (was 4). Why: matches the 5-day rescue wait.
- 2026-11-20: Names: the founder is Ram Altman (was Loki), the AI is CHUD, short for Continuous Helping Unit Deployment. Why: team choice.
- 2026-11-20: Tech stack: 2.5D pre-rendered Blender (Cycles) stills plus Blender MCP, Vite + TypeScript + PixiJS frontend, Howler.js or Web Audio, Node (Express or Hono) /turn backend, Vercel. Why: matches how FNAF 4 was built (pre-rendered stills in a 2D engine), AI coding tools are strongest at web code, lowest 36-hour risk. Godot and real-time 3D rejected.
- 2026-11-21: Adopted the lighthouse visual style guide and designated the local screenshot folder private and gitignored. Why: keep concept art consistent and keep third-party screenshots out of the repo.
- 2026-11-21: Adopted the team whiteboard layout, with CHUD in front of the ocean view, a left entry window, a right stairwell to the ground door, and a rear balcony door. Why: the four views follow the player's position in the room and checking the balcony requires looking behind the chair.
- 2026-11-21: Gameplay rules locked in docs/GAME_LOGIC.md (wins over GAME_DESIGN.md on rule conflicts). Changes vs the design doc: night 1 uses 2 entries (stairs and window), quick command buttons added (fast but do not count as talking), holding has a 6 s limit and 4 s cooldown, the Furious episode lasts one visit then resets to 80, and commands are parsed by code first so the game works even if the LLM is slow. Why: removes solvable or unfair cases and keeps the loop fast.
- 2026-11-21: Each game scene gets its own folder under scenes/ holding its sketch, Blender file, renders and a scene sheet with dialogue. Why: keeps art and story for each scene in one place.
- 2026-11-21: The player is a boat captain with a crew. Scene 1 opens first person at the wheel in a storm, the crew is lost and the boat capsizes. Why: team's starting-scene sketch and outline. Rescue wait (7 vs 5 days) still open.
- 2026-11-21: Rescue wait stays 5 days in the scene 1 coast guard line (not 7). Why: matches the 5 nights.
- 2026-11-21: Limited the schematics to the upper watch room, moved the chair closer to CHUD, and replaced the right stairs with a fixed ladder under a floor hatch. Why: the player can defend the ladder entrance by bracing the lid from inside the room.
- 2026-11-21: Watch room enlarged to 10 m inner diameter and 4 m ceiling (was 6 m and 3 m). Furniture, doors and hatch stay real-world size. Why: the room should feel big, with the entries far apart.
- 2026-11-21: One travel time for every entry, TRAVEL_TIME = 1.5 s, shown as a FNAF 4-style dash (camera tips to the floor, shakes, rises into a close-up hold view). Why: hides the real distances in the bigger room, keeps every fairness rule intact, simpler code.
- 2026-11-21: Game logic prototype in prototype/ (Vite + TypeScript, no art): pure engine first, then a canvas UI, then Claude Haiku as judge and voice through a local server. Commands found by the code parser run at once; the judge result only adds social effects. Why: test the game loop before art is ready, and keep commands fast even when the LLM is slow.
- 2026-11-21: Prototype UI built on the final stack (PixiJS canvas + DOM HUD + Web Audio) so the prototype grows into the game. Every image is an art slot: a coded placeholder until a render with the slot name lands in prototype/assets/. Why: iterative development, art and gameplay added a little at a time instead of a final rush.
- 2026-11-21: LLM is Claude Haiku 5.5 for both judge and voice (team key in prototype/.env). The voice only words conversational lines; locks, beam, location claims and questions stay canned and instant. Why: measured judge 1.2 to 2.2 s and voice under 1 s, fine for conversation but too slow for urgent lines.
- 2026-11-21: Twist ending: Ram is a monster controlled by the AI, used to scare the player into staying. If the player will not stay willingly, the AI tries to kill them. Both endings finish in the Cage Hall full of earlier victims (lose: you wake in a cage, win: you see it from outside). Why: team idea, makes the whole game a cycle of AI gaslighting. Replaces the 2026-11-20 rule that the AI does not want the player dead. Details still open.
- 2026-11-21: The caged monsters are former keepers who lost. Losing turns the player into the next monster the AI controls. Why: makes the cycle explicit and ties the lose ending to Ram.
- 2026-11-21: Scene 1 ends with the player blinking in the water and vaguely seeing a smiling face in the lighthouse's lantern room, the same face as CHUD's display. Why: first hint of the AI, treats the lighthouse as a body with a face.
- 2026-11-21: Each night starts with power on BEAM, not NONE. Lore: at night the generator feeds the lighthouse beacon by default, so every lock steals power from the light. Locks are magnetic, shown on a dashboard on CHUD's screen (design later). Why: explains the one-power-slot rule in the story.
- 2026-11-21: The beacon sits in the lantern room above the watch room. While power = BEAM, its amber sweep crosses the sea through the front glazing every few seconds (rendered loop, built in the lighting and final render phases). Why: keeps the balcony path clear and gives the player a visual readout of the power slot.
- 2026-11-21: Kept pre-rendered stills (FNAF style) over real-time 3D, with looping rendered clips for the windows and entry animations. Why: best look in the time left, browsers have no hardware ray tracing.
- 2026-11-21: CHUD terminal layout from the team sketch: chat in the center, top-down lighthouse map with entrances on the right, clock top right, music player with creepy inserts on the left, power bar bottom right. Why: one screen for talking, checking entries and power. Quick buttons, kill switch and CHUD face placement still open.
- 2026-11-21: The CHUD terminal power bar shows which one thing the power slot feeds (window, hatch, balcony lock or beam). Power never drains. Why: keep the original one-power-slot gameplay from GAME_LOGIC.md.
- 2026-11-21: CHUD's face sits on the left of the terminal, above the music player, using Vidush's four-mood sprite sheet (WARM, HURT, JEALOUS, FURIOUS). The scene 1 lighthouse face uses the same sheet. Why: one face design everywhere, and the player reads CHUD's mood at a glance.
- 2026-11-21: Entry tells changed. Balcony: the door handle is held down for the whole attempt, with a shadow of feet under the door (no rail silhouette, the door is solid). Window: a hand on the glass, part of the face in the hold view. Hatch: fingers over the rim only if the hatch is unlocked, otherwise the lid jolts and bangs. Why: each tell is one clear, readable sign and works with the built room.
- 2026-11-21: BEAM powers the lights circuit (beacon plus the watch room's caged bulbs). Powering a lock darkens the room to CHUD's screen only, and tells become faint. Why: makes "turn the lights on to check" work the same at all three entries and makes locking a frightening choice.
- 2026-11-21: Prototype 3.0 draws each view as the team render (960 x 480 plate) panned by the mouse, warped and night-graded in PixiJS, with CHUD's terminal as DOM over the desk screen and the quick buttons and kill switch inside it. Why: the 2.5D look from stills, with crisp readable text and the GAME_LOGIC 6.3 buttons on the desk screen.
- 2026-11-21: The player can pause during a night with Esc or a pause button (and the game auto-pauses when the tab is hidden). Everything freezes, the scene is dimmed almost black, and the menu offers Resume and Restart night. Why: the player asked for it, and the dimming stops pausing from being used to study the scene for cues.
- 2026-11-21: A breach is an instant jumpscare: the entry's opening burst (a few frames, no Ram) cuts straight to that entry's jumpscare clip, with a snap turn to the entry if the player is looking elsewhere. Why: the scare lands harder with no pause, and no extra "Ram in the doorway" shots are needed.
- 2026-11-21: Dropped the "never show Ram fully" rule. How much of him shows is judged per shot. Why: owner decision; the jumpscare shows him in full.
- 2026-11-21: Adopted FNAF 4 style mouse controls after playtest: hold a screen edge to turn, click to run to an entry, hold the left button to brace, the return edge to run back, keyboard only for chat (GAME_LOGIC.md section 6, new STANDING position, BRACE_REST 1 s). Why: the team liked it in playtest, a player learns it in seconds, and typing to CHUD can never collide with movement keys.
- 2026-11-21: New loop: Ram is silent and never speaks (decoy house noises from a separate random stream instead), CHUD never mentions him (night 1 only warns vaguely of dangerous things at night), 5 minute nights with 3 to 4 visits on night 1 up to 7 to 8 on night 5, attempt windows of at least 5 s, an undefended attempt lets him in silently and the scare comes when you light his entry and look, put down a desk paper, or by a rolled trigger (6 AM with him inside loses), one light per entry held on Shift (the player's own, CHUD can only force it off), locked = blind, and CHUD can hijack a lock request with a malicious smile instead of asking gate questions. Why: constant fear at low frequency for tension, and you find him by looking or by asking CHUD, never by listening.
- 2026-11-21: CHUD speaks its final dialogue through local Piper using the mood attached to each line. Text and the chime stay immediate so synthesis cannot delay gameplay cues.
- 2026-11-21: Adopted THE KEEPER screens on the new loop: skippable shipwreck before the name prompt, automatic midnight and dawn cards, Esc pause, jumpscare into black with Title, no voice logs, and Continue from the highest unlocked night. Why: keep transitions short and preserve progress between sessions.
- 2026-11-21: The final Blender renders are full plates swapped by state (entry view, tell, approach, inside, and 8 hold plates per entry with the arms rendered in), shown alone with no night grade, instead of transparent overlays composited on one view; coded overlays stay only as the fallback for a missing plate. Why: Ram's lighting and the entry light interact correctly when rendered together, which overlays cannot reproduce.
- 2026-11-21: Reduced the gameplay HUD to the clock, night, transient captions and cursor ring, calibrated CHUD to the rendered monitor, disabled desk papers in the UI and moved non-scare clips to on-demand WebM. Why: keep attention on the room and limit browser texture memory.
- 2026-11-22: Release presentation uses final state plates and the designed screens, with optional desk, turn and run footage and a snap into each breach's full scare sequence. Why: the prototype supplies the gameplay, while final art supplies the room and character visuals.
- 2026-11-22: The intro shipwreck now uses one new Blender helm still in the game style for beats 1 to 10, with lightning and the wave and capsize done in code on that still, and the in-game subtitle look for dialogue. Why: the old intro frames were made in a different visual style than the rest of the game.
- 2026-11-22: Use the recorded sound effects and mood-matched CHUD clips through Web Audio, with Piper for custom dialogue. Entry impacts play at the scare reveal. Why: replace temporary cues while preserving the silent entry and immediate subtitles.
- 2026-11-22: New Game includes a safe, skippable evening practice before Night 1, and the desk no longer offers a generator shutdown. Why: teach the actual controls before the threat appears and retain one clear choice between light and lock.
- 2026-11-22: Re-enable desk papers with one tabletop artifact per night from Nights 1 to 3, in clipping, calendar and diary order. Why: reveal each clue in its own night while reading still leaves the player vulnerable.
- 2026-11-22: Kill code ending replaces the night 5 helicopter win: the player types a kill code on night 5 to kill CHUD and free Ram, and 6 AM without the code is a TRAPPED loss. Why: gives the nights a throughline and explains Ram's split control (half AI, half himself).
- 2026-11-22: Kill code is random per run, found on slips Ram drops at entries he was blocked at, and typed into CHUD's chat instead of a separate input screen. Why: ties the code to the core defend loop and makes the kill feel like a takeover of the AI.
- 2026-11-22: The kill code exit scene uses Vidush's two Blender stills (cage hall, rescue boat) with a slow push-in and captions, instead of a rendered walk. Why: the stills are ready now and fit the existing text-beat ending.
- 2026-11-22: Two endings. Code found: the keeper gets on the coast guard boat and leaves. 5 nights survived without the code: the keeper sees the boat through the window, blacks out, wakes caged in the cage hall, and the run starts over (still a fail). Why: makes the code the only real way out and turns surviving alone into a twist, not a win.
- 2026-11-22: Show the Coast Guard boat after the trapped ending's text, then fade to black before Title. Keep the watch-room ambience playing as the player watches the boat.
- 2026-11-23: Make entry lights independent of the single lock slot and require light to see threats from the desk. Why: checking an entry should clearly show what is there while keeping the lock engaged.
- 2026-11-23: Keep the night caption channel for CHUD and radio dialogue. Why: put control instructions in practice and let clues appear in the room during play.
- 2026-11-23: Give Night 1 one truthful CHUD warning per arrival and make silence dialogue mood-specific. Why: teach the first-night defense loop through CHUD and replace repeated demands to talk.
- 2026-11-23: Replace the practice drills with CHUD's four-line story and staged window and hatch visits. The player learns the loop using the same views and defenses as the nights.
- 2026-11-23: Put one gameplay tip on each midnight card and allow four seconds to read it. The tips teach rules left out of practice.
- 2026-11-23: Follow each jumpscare with one skippable explanation on black based on the actual breach. The player can see which entry failed and why before retrying.
- 2026-11-23: The entry light no longer drops locks and dark desk views show nothing (hold Shift to look); a ChudGPT-led practice night with staged visits, a story setup, night card tips and death reasons replace bottom-screen hints. Why: post-hackathon playtests showed new players could not learn the loop.
- 2026-11-23: Prototype a rotating ceiling beam in a dev-only window lab for approval. Why: verify the gradual reveal and compare it with a physical spotlight before changing the game.
- 2026-11-23: Let continuous defense repel a visit early, with the practice hatch using Night 1's threshold. The final shove and low thud let players feel when it is safe to let go.
- 2026-11-23: Paced ChudGPT around three line classes (question, info, reaction), 15 s question timeouts, typing/speech/gap holds, calm-only long lines, quiet-limit light questions replacing silence-tick remarks, single-line voice interruption with pre-generated Piper clips, and a tighter one-sentence corporate-sinister persona. Why: playtesters did not know when to reply and slow queued TTS talked over them.
